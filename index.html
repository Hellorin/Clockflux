Work hours tracker · Progressive web app

Track your work hours, privately

Clockflux is a lightweight time tracker for your working day. Tap once to check in, tap again to check out, and it keeps the running totals for you — today, this week, and across the year. It works offline, it is free, and every hour you log stays in your own browser.

No sign-up. Nothing leaves your device.

Track your day

A single slide toggle starts and stops the clock, with a live timer for the session in progress. Clockflux adds up every session in the day, shows the total in decimal hours or h:mm — whichever you prefer — and highlights your week in green once you are on track for the days elapsed so far. Weekends and days you have marked off are skipped automatically. Hit your daily or weekly target and it says so.

See the whole month

The calendar gives you a month at a glance: which days you logged, which you took off, and how the hours landed. Open any past day to correct a forgotten check-out, add a session you missed, or mark a stretch of days off in bulk.

Keep track of holiday

Set your annual allowance and Clockflux shows what you have used, what you have accrued so far, and what is still available. Choose whether your days accrue gradually through the year or are available all at once, and the balance prorates automatically if you started partway through the year. If your planned and used days are heading past your allowance, you get a warning before it happens.

Watch your work/life balance

The health view reads your recent weeks and tells you plainly whether you are on track, working under, or drifting into sustained overtime. A cumulative overtime chart shows the running balance against your weekly target, with a breakdown of hours versus target for each of the last several weeks.

Offline and installable

Clockflux is a progressive web app: install it on a phone, tablet, or desktop and it opens like any other app and works with no internet connection. The base app is free, with no account to create and no backend to trust — all of your session data is written to your browser's local storage and never sent anywhere.

Privacy

Everything you track is written to your own browser's local storage and stays there. Clockflux keeps five entries: timeforge for your sessions and days off, timeforgeSettings for your holiday allowance and start date, hoursFormat for whether you prefer decimal or h:mm, timeforgeVisited to remember that you have seen this page, and cookieConsent to remember your answer to the cookie banner below. None of it is sent anywhere, and there is no account and no server holding a copy — which also means that clearing your browser data for this site erases your history for good.

The site can use Vercel Web Analytics to count visits. It records page views and referrers only. It sets no cookies, it does not follow you to other sites, and it never sees the hours you log — that data never leaves your device for it to see. To tell repeat visits apart it derives a hash from your IP address and browser details, which Vercel discards and regenerates each day, so it cannot be used to recognise you over time.

Even though analytics itself sets no cookies, we ask before it loads: the first time you open Clockflux a banner offers Accept or Refuse. Accepting lets analytics load for that visit; refusing keeps it switched off. Either choice is remembered locally as cookieConsent so you are not asked again, and you can revisit this notice at any time to see exactly what that choice does.

Signing in with Google, where that feature is enabled, sets one additional first-party session cookie so the browser stays signed in. That cookie is strictly necessary for sign-in to work and is only ever set if you choose to sign in.
